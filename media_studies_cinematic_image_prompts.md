# Media Studies Cinematic Image Prompts

## Camera

### Low-Angle Shot

Create a realistic cinematic still image for a Media Studies teaching website.

Technique to demonstrate: **Low-Angle Shot**

The image must clearly demonstrate this technique visually, so that a student can understand the idea from the image itself without needing labels, arrows, captions, or diagrams.

House style for the full image set:
A coherent contemporary indie-film / prestige television visual style. Realistic human subjects, believable facial expressions, grounded everyday settings, cinematic composition, subtle 35mm film grain, restrained colour grading, natural skin texture, and dramatic but plausible lighting. The image should feel like a frame from a serious short film rather than a stock photo, poster, advertisement, fantasy image, or glossy blockbuster.

Recurring world:
Use realistic teenage or young adult characters in recognisable everyday environments: school corridors, classrooms, bedrooms, buses, train stations, streets, kitchens, cafés, sports fields, libraries, car parks, suburban houses, rehearsal rooms, or urban night locations. The world should feel consistent across the image set: modern, grounded, emotionally observant, and slightly dramatic.

Technique instruction:
Make **Low-Angle Shot** the central visual idea of the image. Place the camera below the subject looking upward, making the character appear powerful, intimidating, heroic, or dominant.

Narrative feel:
The image should suggest a small story moment: tension, isolation, confidence, suspicion, embarrassment, grief, confrontation, anticipation, secrecy, joy, pressure, or discovery. Avoid random posing. The image should look like it belongs inside a scene.

Restrictions:
No text.
No captions.
No arrows.
No diagrams.
No watermarks.
No visible camera crew.
No educational poster layout.
No fantasy imagery unless the technique is surreal imagery.
No exaggerated AI gloss.
No perfect plastic skin.
No random extra fingers or distorted faces.
No logos or copyrighted characters.
No split-screen unless the technique specifically requires it.
No black frame unless the technique is fade to black.

Output:
A single high-resolution landscape cinematic still, 16:9 aspect ratio, suitable for a clean Media Studies website.
